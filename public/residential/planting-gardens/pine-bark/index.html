Choosing the grade

10, 14 or 25 mm: which pine bark goes where.

Pine bark is graded by the size of the pieces, and the grade changes how the bed looks and how it behaves in rain. We pick it bed by bed.

GradeLooks likeUse it forWatch for
10 mmFine, even, almost a carpetPots, planter boxes, narrow strips beside paths and under hedgesBreaks down fastest, so it is topped up sooner
14 mmNeat mid-size nuggetMost front and rear garden beds. The default on a Standard quoteNeeds a hard edge to stay off lawn and paving

How it performs

What pine bark does well, and where we use something else.

It lasts. Bark breaks down more slowly than leaf or chipped green waste, so a bed keeps its depth and colour for longer between top-ups. Expect to add a thin layer every couple of years, not every season.

It stays tidy. The pieces are uniform in size and colour and weather to a soft brown. That is why it is the usual choice for the beds people see from the street.

It feeds the soil slowly. Because it breaks down slowly, it adds less to the soil than leaf mulch or forest fines. On beds where building soil matters more than a crisp finish, we use those instead.

Where it is the wrong choice

Slopes, the house wall and wet corners.

Sloping beds. Bark is light and can wash downhill in heavy rain. On a slope we use a shredded mulch that knits together, or step the bed with edging.

Against the house. No organic mulch should sit against the wall or cover weepholes. We keep it clear of the building and use pebbles in that strip. See how high the garden can sit against the house.

Beds that stay wet. Mulch holds moisture, which is the point in summer and a problem where water already sits. Drainage is fixed first.

Common questions

Asked before nearly every job.

  • Is Pine Bark right for natives?

    Leaf mulch and forest fines suit native beds best; pine bark, chip and cypress are fine on most. Dyed chips are inert and safe but add nothing to the soil. We match mulch to the planting.

  • Can I mix tiers in one yard?

    Yes. A premium soil in the feature bed and standard elsewhere is common and is priced bed by bed.

  • How much do I need?

    We measure the beds at the site visit and the quote carries the volume. Nothing to calculate.

  • How long does pine bark mulch last?

    Longer than most organic mulches. It breaks down slowly, so most beds need a thin top-up every couple of years rather than a full replacement. Fine 10 mm bark goes sooner than 25 mm.

  • Does pine bark take nitrogen from the soil?

    Only at the surface where the bark touches the soil, and not enough to trouble established shrubs. We lay it over a prepared, fed bed and never dig it in.

  • Does pine bark attract termites?

    Any timber mulch can shelter termites if it is piled against a building. Kept clear of the house wall and below the weepholes it is not a risk to the house, which is why we use pebbles in the strip beside the wall.

  • Do you put weed mat under pine bark?

    No. Weed mat under organic mulch stops it feeding the soil and weeds root into the layer on top within a couple of seasons. Depth of mulch and dense planting do the weed control.
