Sir Walter vs Kikuyu: choosing turf for a Sydney yard

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 28 August 2026

The honest comparison we give clients — shade, wear, invasiveness, and what actually decides which lawn thrives.

Sir Walter Buffalo: the family default

Handles part shade down to roughly 3–4 hours of direct sun, wears well, and stays where you put it — it won’t invade garden beds the way runners-first grasses do. Softer underfoot, good winter colour for Sydney, and the reason it’s the default on most of our residential packages. The trade: it costs more per roll than Kikuyu and repairs itself more slowly after damage.

Kikuyu: the full-sun workhorse

Cheapest, fastest to establish, and repairs itself aggressively — a dog-and-kids lawn in full sun is Kikuyu’s home ground. The same vigour is its flaw: it runs into beds, paths and the neighbour’s yard, and it sulks in shade. Choose it with edging you’ll actually maintain.

Couch, briefly

Fine-leafed and show-lawn tight in full sun with regular mowing, but the least shade-tolerant of the three. It suits the front lawn you manicure, not the backyard you live on.

What actually decides it

Count the hours of direct sun in the worst part of the lawn, not the best; be honest about traffic; and remember that no variety fixes a bad base. Whichever turf you choose, it’s laid over screened underlay at proper depth with water available for establishment — that’s most of the difference between a lawn that thrives and one you replace in two years.
