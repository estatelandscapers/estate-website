Do you need council approval for a retaining wall in NSW?

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 28 August 2026

The 600 mm rule of thumb, when engineering is required, and the paperwork your certifier will ask for before an Occupation Certificate.

The rule of thumb

In NSW, retaining walls up to 600 mm can often be built as exempt development — no approval needed — provided they meet the exempt-development conditions, which include distances from boundaries and not being built over services. Above that height, or where a wall supports a driveway, structure or a neighbour’s land, approval and engineering input generally come into play. The thresholds are set by state policy but applied by your council, and sites differ — so treat 600 mm as the point where you start asking questions, not as a guarantee either way.

When engineering is required

Height is only one trigger. A wall that carries surcharge — a driveway above it, a pool nearby, a building footing, or the neighbour’s yard — needs a structural engineer’s design regardless of looking “small”. The design specifies pier depths and spacing, steel, concrete strength and drainage, and the engineer inspects at hold points during construction: typically piers and steel before any concrete is poured.

The paperwork that matters at OC

If your Occupation Certificate depends on the wall, your certifier will ask for the engineer’s certification of the completed work. That certificate only exists if the inspections happened during the build — it cannot be honestly created afterwards without exposing the footings. It’s the single most common reason landscape work delays an OC, and the easiest to avoid: engage the engineering before the wall starts, not after the certifier asks.

What we do about all this

Every wall we quote states whether it needs engineering and what certification is included. On engineered walls, the design, hold-point inspections and final certificate are line items in the job — handed over ready for your certifier.
