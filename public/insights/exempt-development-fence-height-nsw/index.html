Insights · Exempt development

How high can a front and side fence be without approval in NSW?

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 7 November 2026

Fence height is the exempt rule everyone half-knows. The numbers are simple; the catches are masonry, corner blocks, slopes and the strip between the house and the street.

A summary, not advice

This article is a plain-English summary of the exempt development rules in the NSW State Environmental Planning Policy (Exempt and Complying Development Codes) 2008 (the Codes SEPP) as we understand them at the date of publishing. It is generic information: it may or may not apply to your property, the rules change, and your lot can carry conditions (heritage, bushfire, flood, easements, covenants) that take it outside the exempt rules. Read the current code on the NSW legislation website and the NSW Planning development types guide, or check with your council or a certifier. For expert advice and interpretation of the code for your block, email enquiry@estatelandscapers.com.au or call 0414 147 008.

Residential zones (R1 to R4 and RU5)

Under the General Exempt Development Code (fences in certain residential zones), a fence is exempt development if it meets all of these:

  • Side and rear boundary fences: not higher than 1.8 m, or 1.2 m if built from masonry
  • Front fences, and any fence within the setback between the house and a road: not higher than 1.2 m. On a corner block that includes the side boundary facing the second street
  • on a secondary road frontage, the fence is at least 20% transparent above 400 mm, with no solid piers or posts wider than 350 mm
  • corner blocks can have a solid fence up to 1.8 m along the rear 50% of the secondary frontage
  • on a slope, a stepped fence may not exceed 1.5 m per step where 1.2 m applies, or 2.2 m per step where 1.8 m applies
  • gates do not open outwards over the footpath
  • metal parts are low-reflective, factory pre-coloured; no barbed wire or electric fencing in residential zones
  • on bushfire prone land, the fence is non-combustible or hardwood

A fence is not exempt on a lot with a heritage item, within the road setback in a heritage conservation area, or in foreshore areas and on flood control lots.

Rural, environment protection and R5 zones

Fences up to 1.8 m, built as post and wire or post and rail. Masonry is allowed within 3 m either side of the entrance from the primary road. Electric fences must meet AS/NZS 3014.

What is not covered

  • Pool fences: a swimming pool barrier is governed by the Swimming Pools Act and AS 1926.1, not by the fence heights above. See our pool fence article.
  • Retaining walls under a fence: a fence on top of a retaining wall is two structures. The wall has its own exempt limits (600 mm), and the fence height is measured from the ground on the higher side.
  • Who pays: exempt status says nothing about cost. Dividing fence costs are shared under the Dividing Fences Act, which our boundary article covers.
  • Estate covenants in new subdivisions often fix the fence type, colour and front-fence height more tightly than the code.

When a fence needs council

A 1.8 m front fence for privacy on a busy road, a masonry front wall above 1.2 m, or any fence on a heritage lot is not exempt. The route is a complying development certificate where the Housing Code allows it, or a development application. Councils then apply their own development control plan, which in the Hills and the North Shore usually asks for open or part-open front fences.

Questions people ask

  • Can my side fence be 1.8 m from the front boundary all the way back?

    No. The part of the side fence that sits between the front of the house and the street is in the setback, so it is limited to 1.2 m. From the building line back it can be 1.8 m.

  • Does a lattice or slat topper count in the height?

    Yes. Height is measured to the top of the fence including any topper, from the ground on the higher side of the fence.

  • Can I build a 1.8 m Colorbond fence on a corner block?

    Along the rear half of the secondary street frontage, yes. Along the front half and the primary frontage it is 1.2 m and must be partly transparent.

  • Does my neighbour have to agree to a higher fence?

    Agreement affects who pays, not whether it is exempt. If you want more than a sufficient dividing fence, you pay the extra and the height rules still apply.
