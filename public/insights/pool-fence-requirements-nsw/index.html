Pool fence rules in NSW: heights, gates and the non-climbable zone

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 7 November 2026

A pool is never exempt development; it needs a complying development certificate or a DA. The barrier around it has its own rules, set by the Swimming Pools Act 1992 and Australian Standard AS 1926.1, and those rules are checked at sale, at lease and on inspection. This is what the barrier has to do.

A summary, not advice

This article is a plain-English summary of the exempt development rules in the NSW State Environmental Planning Policy (Exempt and Complying Development Codes) 2008 (the Codes SEPP) as we understand them at the date of publishing. It is generic information: it may or may not apply to your property, the rules change, and your lot can carry conditions (heritage, bushfire, flood, easements, covenants) that take it outside the exempt rules. Read the current code on the NSW legislation website and the NSW Planning development types guide, or check with your council or a certifier. For expert advice and interpretation of the code for your block, email enquiry@estatelandscapers.com.au or call 0414 147 008.

Which rules apply

Pools built or barriers installed from 1 May 2013 are assessed against AS 1926.1-2012. Older pools are checked against the standard in force when they were built (AS 1926-1986 before September 2008, AS 1926.1-2007 to April 2013), but councils encourage upgrading, and some older exemptions fall away when the house is altered. Every pool or spa that can hold 300 mm of water or more must be registered on the NSW Swimming Pool Register.

The barrier, in numbers

For a barrier assessed to AS 1926.1-2012, the checklist used by the NSW Swimming Pool Register asks for:

  • a fence at least 1200 mm high, measured on the outside
  • a gap under the fence of no more than 100 mm, and vertical bars less than 100 mm apart
  • a 900 mm non-climbable zone: nothing a child can get a hand or foot onto within 900 mm of the top of the fence, measured on the outside, and a 300 mm clear zone on the inside
  • perforated or mesh fencing with holes of 13 mm or less
  • a gate that opens outwards, away from the pool, is self-closing and self-latching from any position, with the latch release at least 1500 mm above the ground (or, if inside the gate, at least 1200 mm up and 150 mm below the top of the gate)
  • a boundary fence used as part of the barrier that is at least 1800 mm high measured on the inside, with its own non-climbable zone at the top
  • no door from the house opening directly into the pool area on newer pools, and a CPR sign readable from 3 m

Landscaping is where barriers fail. A garden bed, a retaining wall, a pergola post, a raised planter or a tree within the 900 mm zone turns a compliant fence into a non-compliant one. We set out planting, walls and paving around the pool with the zone drawn on the plan, not after the fence is in.

Exempt, complying or DA

  • The pool is approved through a complying development certificate (the Housing Code allows pools on most residential lots, with limits on setbacks, decking and pumps) or a development application.
  • The barrier is normally approved and inspected as part of the pool approval. The Codes SEPP also contains provisions for fences for swimming pools in residential zones, so a barrier built to the Act and the Standard around an existing pool can generally be installed without a separate DA; confirm the current clause with your certifier.
  • A certificate of compliance from council or a private certifier is required before a property with a pool is sold or leased. It is valid for three years. On a sale a certificate of non-compliance can be used instead, giving the buyer 90 days to fix the barrier; on a lease it cannot.

Questions people ask

  • Can the house wall or a boundary fence be part of the barrier?

    A boundary fence can, at 1800 mm measured inside with a non-climbable zone. A house wall can only where no door opens into the pool area and any windows meet the standard; newer pools cannot rely on child-safe doors.

  • Does glass pool fencing meet the standard?

    Frameless glass is widely used and compliant when the panels, gaps, gate hardware and height meet AS 1926.1. The usual failures are gate latches that do not self-latch and spigots or planters that create a foothold.

  • What if the pool is on a sloping block?

    The 1200 mm is measured from the finished ground on the outside. Where the ground rises against the fence, the fence rises with it, or the retaining is cut back so the measured height holds.

  • Who inspects the barrier?

    Council or a private certifier registered for pool inspections. Councils in Sydney run inspection programs, and a certificate is required at sale and lease regardless.
