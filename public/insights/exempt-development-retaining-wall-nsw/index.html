Can I build a front retaining wall without approval in NSW?

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 7 November 2026

A low wall at the front of a block is the most common retaining job we quote, and the most common question is whether it needs council. Often it does not. The Codes SEPP lets a small retaining wall be built as exempt development if every standard is met. Miss one and it is not exempt, whatever the height.

A summary, not advice

This article is a plain-English summary of the exempt development rules in the NSW State Environmental Planning Policy (Exempt and Complying Development Codes) 2008 (the Codes SEPP) as we understand them at the date of publishing. It is generic information: it may or may not apply to your property, the rules change, and your lot can carry conditions (heritage, bushfire, flood, easements, covenants) that take it outside the exempt rules. Read the current code on the NSW legislation website and the NSW Planning development types guide, or check with your council or a certifier. For expert advice and interpretation of the code for your block, email enquiry@estatelandscapers.com.au or call 0414 147 008.

What "exempt development" means

Exempt development is work the NSW Government has decided is minor enough to be built without a development application or a complying development certificate, provided it meets every standard written in the code for that type of work. The standards are a checklist, not a guide: if the wall fails one, it is not exempt, and it needs a complying development certificate or a DA before it is built.

The standards for a retaining wall

Under the General Exempt Development Code (earthworks, retaining walls and structural support), a retaining wall can be exempt if it:

  • is not more than 600 mm high, measured vertically from the base of the wall to its top
  • is at least 2 m (measured horizontally) from any other retaining wall or structural support on the lot, so two low walls cannot be stacked into one tall terrace
  • is at least 1 m from any registered easement, sewer main or water main
  • is set back from the lot boundary (the code sets 1 m for exempt earthworks; some councils quote 900 mm for the wall itself, so check the current clause for your zone)
  • has adequate drainage connected to the existing stormwater system, and does not redirect surface water or groundwater onto a neighbour
  • is structurally adequate, and installed to the manufacturer’s specifications where there are any
  • is not on a lot that contains a heritage item, and in a heritage conservation area is in the rear yard only
  • is not in an environmentally sensitive area and not on a flood control lot

The same subdivision limits any cut or fill to 600 mm above or below existing ground level, and where fill is deeper than 150 mm it may cover no more than 25% of the lot. Imported fill must be virgin excavated natural material, free of building waste.

So, the front yard

Nothing in the exempt standards says a retaining wall must be at the back. A 600 mm wall across a front garden, set back from the boundary, services and the footpath, with its drainage tied in, can be exempt. The exceptions are heritage conservation areas (rear yard only) and lots with site-specific controls. The front wall also has to work with the driveway and the landscaped-area rules that apply to paving at the front of the block.

When it is no longer exempt

The wall needs engineering and an approval pathway when any of these apply:

  • it is taller than 600 mm, or a second wall sits within 2 m of it
  • it supports a driveway, a building, a pool or a neighbour’s fill
  • it is on or near the boundary, an easement or a sewer
  • it is part of a larger cut and fill of the block

From there the options are a complying development certificate (a certifier checks the wall against the Housing Code, which allows taller engineered walls in residential zones) or a development application to council. Either way the wall is designed by a structural engineer, and that design is what we build from. Our retaining wall approval guide walks through the pathways.

Questions people ask

  • Does a 600 mm wall still need an engineer?

    Not under the exempt code, but it must be structurally adequate. We build low walls to the sleeper or block manufacturer’s tables, with drainage behind them, because a 600 mm wall holding wet clay carries real load.

  • Can I step two 600 mm walls up a slope?

    Only if they are at least 2 m apart measured horizontally. Closer than that and the pair is treated as one structure, which is no longer exempt.

  • My block is in a new estate with a covenant. Does exempt still apply?

    The planning rules may say exempt, but a covenant or an estate design guideline is a separate private obligation. Check both before building.

  • Who pays for a wall on the boundary?

    Walls on the boundary are not exempt in most cases and raise a cost question with the neighbour. Our boundary wall and fence article covers it.
