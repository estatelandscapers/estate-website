Six commitments

What "built properly" means, specifically.

  1. Set-out and levels first

    Boundaries, falls and finished levels are established before anything is built. Nearly every expensive landscape rework traces back to this step being skipped or rushed — so it never is.

  2. Drainage before surfaces

    Ag lines, pits and falls go in before turf, paving or concrete goes down. Retrofitting drainage means pulling up the finished work to reach it.

  3. Engineering where it's needed

    Walls and structures that carry load get a structural design, hold-point inspections and certification — the documents your certifier or superintendent will ask for.

  4. Materials that survive year one

    Certified turf over proper underlay, compacted base under everything paved, weed mat under decorative stone. The cheap version of each looks the same for about a season.

  5. One crew, one contact

    The package is self-performed by our crews wherever possible, with one person answering for the whole job — so nothing falls between trades, and neither do you.

  6. Documented handover

    Progress photos through the build, engineer certificates where they apply, and care notes for the first season. The job isn't finished when the tools leave — it's finished when the paperwork does.

What it means for price

We're rarely the cheapest quote. Here's exactly why.

When quotes for the same yard are thousands apart, the difference is almost never margin — it's what's underneath. Piering on the wall. Drainage behind it. The base under the driveway. Turf underlay. An itemised quote shows every element, so you can see what you're comparing instead of guessing.

If another quote is cheaper, ask for it itemised and put them side by side. Where ours is higher, you'll be able to see the line it's higher on — and decide whether that line matters to you. Usually it's the one you'd otherwise pay for twice.
