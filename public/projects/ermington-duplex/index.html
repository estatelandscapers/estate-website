The brief

Scope moved to where it was solved better.

A duplex development in Ermington, still under construction. The boundary retaining wall sat in the builder's scope — until we proposed a better answer: relocate the wall on the boundary so it opened genuine access down the side of the property, and deliver it for better value than the original arrangement. The builder handed the scope across, and we picked up the wall along with the full landscape package.

Landscape scope delivered with it: soil relocated and levelled; 262 m² of certified Sir Walter Buffalo over screened underlay; 66 m of 1.8 m Colorbond with four gates; a black blade fence to the street; 70 m of aluminium and timber edging; weedmat and river-pebble access paths; premium garden mix and mulched beds — down to the letterboxes and clotheslines, because "complete" should mean complete.

Photo slotSide access created by the relocated wall

The engineering

Restricted access changed the wall — so we changed the certification path.

The constraint. Access on a live duplex site is tight, and the wall runs 41 metres to 1.2 metres high. Conventional systems needed room we didn't have without disrupting the builder's program.

The solution. FRC (fibre-reinforced concrete) sleepers, which could be handled and placed within the access available. Because that departs from the usual sleeper arrangement, we worked with our structural engineers to have the system designed and approved under AS 3600, the concrete structures standard — rather than assuming it would be accepted.

The coordination. The works were arranged with City of Parramatta and Housing NSW, so a wall on the boundary satisfied everyone with an interest in it before it was built, not after.

The result. Approximately 60 tonnes excavated and disposed, ag-line drainage with aggregate backfill behind the wall, and engineer sign-off on completion — built safely alongside the builder finishing their internal works, with no delay to the owner moving in.
