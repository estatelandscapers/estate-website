Sandstone log retaining walls

Solid sandstone logs, cut from Sydney basin stone and set in courses on a compacted base. The natural-stone option in our Premium tier: permanent, heavy, and at home on acreage and in established gardens.

Photo slotSandstone log wall, full length, in an established garden

How it is built

Logs are typically around two metres long and 300 mm square, weighing several hundred kilograms each, so they are placed by machine. The first course sits on a compacted road-base bed below finished grade; each course above is set back slightly and pinned or mortared where the design calls for it. Ag-line drainage and free-draining backfill sit behind the wall, exactly as with any other system.

Where it makes sense

Garden terracing, level changes on larger blocks, native and acreage landscapes, and anywhere a manufactured block would look wrong. Because the stone is the finish, there is nothing to render, paint or replace. We built terraced sandstone log walls into the Dural acreage project, where they carry the level changes through native planting.

Honest limits

Sandstone is a gravity wall: it relies on mass and setback, so height is limited without engineering. From 600 mm and above, the wall is engineered, as with every wall we build. Access matters too; the machine that places the logs needs a way in. And sandstone is porous, so drainage behind it is not optional.
