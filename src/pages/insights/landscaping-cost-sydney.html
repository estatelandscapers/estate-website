What landscaping costs in Sydney, and how to set a budget that holds

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 20 November 2026

Most landscaping budgets are set by guesswork, then broken by the first quote. National cost guides do not help much: they average a courtyard refresh in one city with an engineered hillside in another. This guide uses our own rates for complete landscape projects in Sydney, so you can build a realistic number before anyone visits the site.

A guide, not a quote

The figures here are indicative ranges based on our standard rates at the date of publishing, including GST. They are general information. Your site, access, levels, soil and choice of finishes change the price, and rates move with material and labour costs. Nothing on this page is an offer or a fixed price. What these rates do not include

  • Plants, shrubs and trees. Priced from the planting plan, species by species. Mature trees and hedging can be a significant line on their own.
  • Retaining walls over 1.2 m. Every wall above 600 mm needs an engineer's sign-off, and that is part of a normal job. Walls above 1.2 m are a different class of work: they are not standard construction, they need heavy engineering and may need a geotechnical report, and they are priced to that design.
  • Drainage. It depends on the levels and where the water can lawfully go, so it is priced after the site visit.
  • Decks, pergolas, lighting, irrigation and design fees. Priced per project.
  • Approvals and engineering fees where the work needs them. We tell you what applies when we quote.

How to set a budget that holds

  1. Budget the whole yard, even if you build in stages. Levels, drainage and retaining have to be right for everything that follows. Staging the finishes is sensible. Staging the groundwork means digging up finished work later.
  2. Put the money in the ground first. Earthworks, drainage, walls and concrete cannot be upgraded later without demolition. Turf, mulch and plants can.
  3. Hold 10 to 15 per cent back. Rock, soft ground and services in the wrong place are found when digging starts. A contingency turns a surprise into a decision.
  4. Mix the tiers. Premium finishes at the front door and the entertaining area, Basic along the side of the house. This is the most effective way to bring a quote down without losing the result.
  5. Budget for landscaping when you sign the building contract. It is rarely in the build price, and it is often needed for the Occupation Certificate.

How payments are staged

You pay for stages as they are delivered. Our standard schedule on a full package is 10 per cent on acceptance, 20 per cent on site establishment, 30 per cent at half way, 30 per cent at 80 per cent complete and 10 per cent at practical completion. Any change to the scope is quoted in writing and only proceeds with your approval. On residential work over $20,000 we take out Home Building Compensation Fund cover and give you the certificate before work starts.

Questions people ask

  • How much does landscaping cost for a new build in Sydney?

    On a standard level block, a complete package of retaining, driveway, fencing, turf and garden beds is typically $45,000 to $80,000 with us, depending on the finishes. Plants are priced from the plan on top of that.

  • How much does a retaining wall cost?

    It depends on the height, the length, the material and what the wall has to hold up. A rendered block wall costs roughly twice what a timber sleeper wall of the same size does. Any wall above 600 mm needs an engineer's sign-off. Walls above 1.2 m are not standard work: they need heavy engineering, possibly a geotechnical report, and are priced to that design. The online estimate gives a range for your length of wall.

  • How much does a concrete driveway cost?

    The finish is the biggest factor. Exposed aggregate costs roughly twice as much as plain concrete, with colour concrete in between. The size of the pour, the base preparation and access for the truck also change the price.

  • Why are landscaping quotes so far apart?

    Usually because they are not pricing the same job. Check that each quote includes earthworks, removal, drainage, engineering and the same finishes. An itemised quote makes the comparison possible.

  • Can you give me a price without a site visit?

    A ballpark, yes. The online estimate asks about a dozen plain questions and gives a range from Basic to Premium in under ten minutes. The itemised quote follows a site visit, within 48 hours.

  • Is the price fixed once I sign?

    The quoted scope is priced line by line. What can change it is something nobody could see beforehand, such as rock, or a change you ask for. Either is quoted in writing as a variation before any extra work is done.
