Why compliance matters — and what it costs when it's skipped.

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 30 August 2026

"Compliance" sounds like paperwork. On a landscape construction project it's the difference between an asset and a liability — and the costs of skipping it don't show up at handover. They show up later, with interest.

What compliance actually means on a landscaping job

Four things, depending on the project: approvals where council or state rules require them (larger retaining walls, structures, significant earthworks); engineering where a wall or structure carries load — designed by a structural engineer, not guessed; inspections and certification at hold points, so an engineer sees the piers and steel before concrete hides them forever; and safe, documented work — SWMS, insurances and licensing that protect everyone on and around the site. None of it is decoration. Each one exists because the failure it prevents is expensive.

What skipping it actually costs

Your Occupation Certificate. On new builds, certifiers routinely ask for engineering certification of retaining walls and compliant driveways before issuing the OC. That certificate can only exist if inspections happened during construction — it cannot be honestly written afterwards without exposing the footings. An uncertified wall is one of the most common reasons families can't move into a finished house on time.

Council orders and rectification. Unapproved works can attract council attention years later — often triggered by a neighbour complaint or a sale. The outcome ranges from retrospective approval costs to orders to modify or demolish the works, at the owner's expense, regardless of what was paid to build them.

Insurance that doesn't pay. When a non-engineered wall fails and damages property — yours or the neighbour's — insurers investigate whether the works were compliant. Unapproved, uncertified construction gives an insurer grounds to walk away, leaving the owner personally exposed for the damage and the rebuild.

The wall itself. Engineering isn't bureaucracy — it's the pier depths, steel and drainage that stop a wall moving. Walls built without it are the ones leaning within a few winters, holding back a driveway or a neighbour's yard while they do it.

Selling the property. Conveyancing increasingly surfaces unapproved structures. Buyers renegotiate, demand rectification, or walk — and the discount they extract is usually a multiple of what compliance would have cost on day one.

Why cheap quotes are often cheap

Engineering, inspections, certification and proper documentation cost real money. A quote that's thousands cheaper often isn't more efficient — it's quietly excluding the compliance, and the saving transfers the risk to you. When you compare quotes, compare what's underneath: ask each contractor, in writing, what engineering and certification is included. The honest ones will tell you line by line.
