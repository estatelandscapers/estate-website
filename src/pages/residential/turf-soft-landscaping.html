Genuine varieties

Certified turf, not a look-alike.

The named varieties above are protected breeds. Buying them through an accredited grower means three things you can hold us to: the turf is AusGAP certified under the Australian Genetic Assurance Program, so what arrives is the variety on the invoice; Sir Walter is DNA certified, which is the only way to know a buffalo is actually Sir Walter; and the varieties carry a ten-year product warranty from the grower network. Uncertified "buffalo" from a roadside sign has none of that, and it is usually the reason a lawn fails to match the yard next door.

Common questions

Asked before nearly every job.

  • Sir Walter or Kikuyu?

    If the budget allows, we push clients toward Sir Grange Zoysia: it grows so slowly it needs a fraction of the mowing, stays dense and soft underfoot, and gives a yard that golf-course feel, kids love it. Sir Walter Buffalo is the reliable middle: shade-tolerant, family-proof, low invasiveness. Kikuyu is the budget workhorse for full sun, but it spreads into beds and needs constant mowing; couch we generally steer people away from for backyards. We recommend per yard, not per fashion.

  • When can I walk on new turf?

    Light traffic after the first couple of weeks; normal use around week four to six depending on season. The care notes at handover give your yard’s specifics.

  • Why did my last lawn fail?

    Usually the base: thin or poor underlay, no levels, no water. We fix the base first, it’s most of the labour and all of the difference.
