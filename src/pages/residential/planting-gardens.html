Common questions

What people ask about soil and mulch.

  • Mulch or wood chip: what is the difference?

    Mulch is the layer; wood chip, bark, leaf and forest fines are all types of it. Chip and bark last longest and feed the soil least; leaf mulch and forest fines break down fastest and feed it most. Choose by how often you want to top up and whether the bed needs feeding.

  • How deep should mulch be?

    50 to 100 mm, measured after it settles. Thinner and weeds come through; thicker and water struggles to reach the soil. Keep it 50 mm clear of plant stems and tree trunks so they do not rot.

  • Is black or red dyed mulch safe?

    Yes. The colour is a non-toxic oxide stain on softwood chip, safe around children and pets. The honest trade-offs: dyed chip is inert so it does not feed the bed, the colour fades within a couple of seasons, and leaf litter shows against black more than against bark.

  • Does mulch attract termites?

    Any timber product can harbour termites if it is piled against the house. Keep mulch below the slab edge and weep holes, leave a gap at the wall, and use cypress near timber structures because its oils resist termites. Regular inspections matter more than the mulch choice.

  • Garden mix or topsoil?

    Topsoil is a base layer with little organic content. Garden mix is soil blended with compost and organics to plant straight into. New builds usually need both: fill and level with topsoil, then garden mix in the beds.

  • Which soil for natives?

    Native Mix. It has no mushroom compost, drains freely and is low in phosphorus, which many natives, azaleas and camellias cannot tolerate. We specify it at any tier wherever the plant schedule calls for it.

  • Why did my plants die in the new bed?

    Usually the soil under the mix: builder clay with no drainage, so roots sit in water. We rip or ameliorate the subsoil and shape falls before the garden mix goes in, which is most of the labour and all of the difference.

  • Can I put new mulch over old?

    Yes, if the old layer has broken down and the total depth stays under 100 mm. Rake off matted or mouldy mulch first, and never bury the base of the plants.

  • Is it worth paying for premium soil?

    On a feature bed with an expensive plant schedule, yes: soil is a small share of the cost and the largest share of the result. On a hedge or screening run, Standard is fine.

  • How much mulch do I need?

    Area in square metres times depth in metres: a 30 m² bed at 75 mm needs about 2.3 m³. We measure and quote it, so there is nothing to calculate.
